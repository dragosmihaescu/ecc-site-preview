In detail

  • 01

    Voluntary carbon credits (verified)

    Units issued by independent standards after verification of a project that reduces or removes greenhouse-gas emissions (for example a renewable-energy, energy-efficiency or forestry project). By convention, one credit corresponds to one tonne of CO₂ equivalent. A voluntary credit is not a permit to emit. Organisations use them voluntarily, to finance emission reductions in addition to their own. Quality varies: the standard, methodology, verifier and retirement of the credit in the registry all matter.

  • 02

    Regulated emission allowances (EU ETS)

    Allowances created under European Union law within the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS). An allowance entitles a covered installation to emit one tonne of CO₂ equivalent and is used for legal compliance. It is a regulated instrument, distinct from voluntary credits, and the two are not interchangeable.

  • 03

    Renewable-energy certificates (guarantees of origin / RECs)

    Electronic documents showing that a quantity of electricity (typically 1 MWh) was generated from renewable sources. In the European Union these are guarantees of origin; other regions use REC-type systems. They evidence the origin of energy, not an emission reduction in themselves, and are distinct from both carbon credits and EU ETS allowances.
